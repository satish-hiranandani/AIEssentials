A Large Language Model (like ChatGPT) is really a giant next-word predictor. It looks at the words so far, guesses the most likely next word, adds it, and repeats. Try it below - you are the model!

How this maps to real LLMs (teacher talking points)

  • Next-word prediction: Real models like ChatGPT do exactly this - predict the next word (token), add it, and repeat - just trained on billions of sentences instead of ~40.
  • Learned from data: The bars come from counting what followed each phrase in the text. No rules were written by hand - that's machine learning.
  • Probabilities, not certainty: It doesn't "know" the answer; it picks from likely options. That's why the same prompt can give different results.
  • Temperature: The "Surprise me" slider is real - LLMs have a temperature setting. Low = safe/predictable, high = creative/random (and more mistakes).
  • Context window: Our model looks at the last 1-2 words. Real LLMs look back over thousands of words - so they stay on topic far better.
  • Hallucinations: Because it only predicts likely words, it can produce fluent text that's wrong. Always verify!
